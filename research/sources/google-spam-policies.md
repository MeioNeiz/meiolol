# Google: Spam policies for Google web search (docs, 2026)

Why: are ~50k generated matchup pages a spam risk?

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Google's own policy page.
- Reasoning: fair. Defines by purpose and value with examples, no thresholds.
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: the test is value to users, not page count or how pages are made; enforcement
  is judgement, so the safe side is pages with real, distinct numbers.

## Findings

- [seo] Scaled content abuse is judged by purpose (ranking manipulation, little value), not by how many pages or how they are made. ^f1
  > Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users.
  > This abusive practice is typically focused on creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created.
- [seo] Its examples are scraped, stitched, synonymised or nonsense pages; none is original data shown per page. ^f2
  > Creating many pages where the content makes little or no sense to a reader but contains search keywords
- [seo] Doorway abuse includes substantially similar pages closer to search results than a browseable hierarchy, the risk for thin matchup pages that differ only in names. ^f3
  > Creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy
- [seo] Checked, not there: no page count, rate or ratio that triggers either policy. ^f4
