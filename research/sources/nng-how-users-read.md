# NN/g: How Users Read on the Web (Nielsen, 1997)

Why: is there measured evidence that cutting words helps, and by how much.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. The canonical study, cited for nearly 30 years and re-confirmed by later
  NN/g eyetracking (see [[nng-f-pattern]]).
- Reasoning: fair. Reports a controlled comparison of five versions of one site with four
  performance measures; sample size and tasks only in the linked full report.
- Interests: NN/g sells the eyetracking report.
- Weigh: the direction is solid; the exact percentages are one 1997 study on a tourism
  site, not a stats UI.

## Findings

- [ux] Most users scan: 79% always scanned a new page, 16% read word by word. Intro ^f1
  > 79 percent of our test users always scanned any new page they came across; only 16 percent read word-by-word.
- [ux] Prescription: keywords highlighted, plain sub-headings, lists, one idea per
  paragraph, conclusion first, half the words. Intro ^f2
  > half the word count (or less) than conventional writing
- [ux] One idea per paragraph, since readers skip ideas not caught by the first words. Intro ^f3
  > users will skip over any additional ideas if they are not caught by the first few words in the paragraph
- [ux] Measured: concise text (about half the words) 58% more usable, scannable layout
  47%, objective language 27%, all three together 124%. Measuring the Effect ^f4
  > measured usability was dramatically higher for the concise version (58% better) and for the scannable version (47% better).
  > when we combined three ideas for improved writing style into a single site, the result was truly stellar: 124% better usability.
- [ux] The concise version had about half the control's word count. Table ^f5
  > with about half the word count as the control condition
- [ux] Boastful or subjective wording costs performance, not only liking: time, errors,
  memory and site structure were all better with neutral wording. Measuring the Effect ^f6
  > our four performance measures (time, errors, memory, and site structure) were also better for the objective version than for the promotional version.
- [ux] (inferred) A stats site's verdict words ("crushes", "hard counter") are the
  promotional case; neutral labels plus the number are the measured better form. ^f7
