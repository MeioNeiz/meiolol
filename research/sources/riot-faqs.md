# Riot Developer Portal FAQs

Why: official answers on what a production application needs.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Canonical Riot FAQ; partly stale (Vanguard "coming early 2024", app messaging
  transition April 2024).
- Reasoning: fair. Explains why a website is needed.
- Interests: Riot.
- Weigh: stated review times ("weekly", "up to three weeks") conflict with 2026 practice
  reports of months; treat as aspirational.

## Findings

- [policy] Production keys need a functioning site/app or prototype showing full execution. ^f1
  > For production keys, we need to see a functioning website/application or a prototype that demonstrates you’ll be able to fully execute on your use case.
- [policy] Official review time: weekly, up to three weeks. ^f2
  > Applications are typically reviewed weekly for the previous week. However, due to overwhelming amounts of requests, sometimes the review process can take up to three weeks.
- [policy] Sending multiple messages can push you to the back of the queue. ^f3
  > Since we review specific date ranges, submitting multiple messages may kick you to the back of the queue.
- [policy] One application per game per product; no multiple apps to bypass limits. ^f4
  > you can have one application per game per product.
  > Please note you may NOT have multiple applications to bypass rate limits.
- [policy] Key approved only for the described use case and game. ^f5
  > Your API key is approved only for the use case and game within your description.
- [policy] RSO only for approved production apps; new approvals get an invite. ^f6
  > RSO Clients are only available for applications that have an existing, approved production application ID. All newly approved production applications will receive a link inviting them to apply for RSO access.
- [policy] Website required: to see what the app does plus Terms of Service and Privacy Policy. ^f7
  > We need to see a website to understand what your app does, view your Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, and to make sure your app is in a good state for a production key.
- [policy] Production keys for completed or near-complete apps. ^f8
  > Production keys are for applications that are in a completed or near-complete state.
- [policy] GitHub repos or source code not accepted in place of a functioning app/site. ^f9
  > we are unable to accept Github repositories and source code in lieu of a functioning application/site.
- [policy] Native apps must be hosted on an owned domain with download, ToS and Privacy Policy. ^f10
  > In most cases, we still require you to host your native app on a domain you own. This is so that users can easily see what your product does, where to download it, where you list your Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, and so we can verify that you’re the owner of the app.
- [policy] Video demos, screenshots or Figma files can demonstrate user flow. ^f11
  > Other ways are video demos, screenshots, or Figma files that clearly demonstrate your user flow.
- [policy] Verification via text string uploaded to your URL; no verified site, no production key. ^f12
  > After submitting your production application, you’ll receive a text string to upload to your URL that proves you’re the website owner.
  > We cannot grant production keys to applications without a verified website.
- [policy] Vanguard: LCU and in-game API apps expected to keep working; memory readers break; no allow list. ^f13
  > Apps developed using the LCU and in-game APIs are still expected to work.
  > No - There is absolutely no allow list for Vanguard.
- [policy] Support site replaced app messaging for most contact (from 26 April 2024); approvals still via app messages. ^f14
  > For now, we’ll still be sending approvals/rejection reasons and RSO instructions in app messages.
- [policy] Checked, not there: no mention of ML, AI, model training, or draft/pick tools. ^f15
