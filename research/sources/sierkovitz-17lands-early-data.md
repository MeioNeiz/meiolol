# Sierkovitz: Early data can be misleading, MH3 Dimir (X thread, Jun 2024)

Why: how do users of another game's stat site (17Lands, MTG Arena drafts) read a new
set's first days?

## Assessment

- Evidence: C. A known 17Lands analyst's first-hand reading of one set's first days
  (Modern Horizons 3, June 2024); one case, numbers quoted from 17Lands, no follow-up in
  the thread on whether Dimir's rate rose.
- Reasoning: fair. Gives the mechanism (who builds the deck early) and the check
  (finer-grained rates inside the aggregate).
- Interests: none known.
- Weigh: the mechanism, not the size.

## Findings

- [method/dynamics] Claim: a new set's early aggregate win rates mislead; the example
  archetype sat at 54%, among the six lowest. ^f1
  > Early data can be misleading. And in MH3 we have a great case study for that.
- [method/dynamics] His evidence that the aggregate understates it: the archetype's own
  commons win far more than those of archetypes with the same aggregate rate (8 commons
  above 59% against 1-5 for the others). ^f2
  > 8 commons have a GiH WR >59%.
- [method/dynamics] Mechanism: early on, badly built decks are most of the archetype's
  games; they lack its good cards, so the cards' rates stay high while the archetype's
  is pulled down. ^f3
  > Bad builds dominate the data. And they cover archetype win rate. But bad builds don’t have the right cards in them, so individual card win rates shine.
- [method/dynamics] (inferred) For us: a changed champion's day-0 win rate mixes old
  builds with the new best one, so the rate on the build that will win out (item or
  keystone cells) may lead the champion-role rate. Our TS3 (behaviour first) asks the
  same from pick and build shares. ^f4
- [stats] Checked, not there: no sample sizes, no interval, no later numbers showing the
  archetype rose. ^f5
