# ICO: European representatives

Why: Jacob is in the UK and the site serves EU (EUW) players: is EU GDPR in play, and
does he need an EU representative?

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Regulator's post-Brexit guidance. Page now 404 on ico.org.uk (removed
  between Dec 2024 and Aug 2026, probably in a site reorganisation); Wayback copy of 27
  Dec 2024. The rule itself is EU GDPR Art 3(2) and 27, which have not changed.
- Reasoning: fair. Clear conditions and a worked example.
- Interests: regulator.
- Weigh: the exemption's "occasional" condition is the open point for a site that
  continuously crawls and shows EU players' data; commentators read it narrowly. EDPB
  Guidelines 3/2018 are the authority on scope (not read).

## Findings

- [privacy] [policy] A UK controller with no EEA establishment that offers services to,
  or monitors the behaviour of, people in the EEA must comply with EU GDPR for that
  processing. ^f1
  > then you still need to comply with the EU GDPR regarding this processing.
- [privacy] [policy] It must appoint, in writing, a representative in an EEA state where
  some of those people are. ^f2
  > the EU GDPR requires you to appoint a representative in the EEA. This representative needs to be set up in an EU or EEA state where some of the individuals whose personal data you are processing in this way are located.
- [privacy] Representative details go in the privacy notice and must be easy for
  regulators to find; no need to notify regulators. ^f3
  > This may be done by including them in your privacy notice or in the upfront information you give them when you collect their data.
- [privacy] Exemption: processing only occasional, low risk, and no large-scale special
  category or criminal data. ^f4
  > your processing is only occasional, of low risk to the data protection rights of individuals, and does not involve the large-scale use of special category or criminal offence data.
- [privacy] Having a representative does not shift liability. ^f5
  > Having a representative does not affect your own responsibility or liability under the EU GDPR.
- [privacy] (inferred) A 24/7 crawler storing EU players' match histories, and a lookup
  site profiling them, is hard to call "occasional", so meiolol probably needs an EEA
  representative (a paid service, as OP.GG uses Bird & Bird, [[opgg-privacy-policy#^f6]])
  once it shows identifiable EU players. Aggregate-only stats would sidestep much of
  this. Whether crawling-plus-profile is "monitoring behaviour" is a further question. ^f6
