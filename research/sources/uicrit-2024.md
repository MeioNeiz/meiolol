# Duan et al. 2024: UICrit (UIST 2024)

Why: how good are LLMs at judging a UI from a screenshot, and what helps.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Peer-reviewed (UIST), Google Research and Berkeley, expert raters with
  p-values; but small user study (6 UIs, 6 experts) and a 2023 model (Gemini Pro Vision).
- Reasoning: fair. Ablations over prompting, admits a large gap to humans.
- Interests: none beyond promoting the dataset.
- Weigh: the direction (examples and a narrow task beat zero-shot; LLM critique still
  below designers) is likely durable; absolute numbers are dated by newer models.

## Findings

- [web/qa] LLM UI evaluation did not match human evaluators. Abstract ^f1
  > However, current LLM-based techniques do not yet match the performance of human evaluators.
- [web/qa] Zero-shot, only 13.1% of Gemini's 5,927 design comments were judged valid.
  4 Dataset analysis ^f2
  > only 13.1 percent of the design comments generated by Gemini were valid.
- [web/qa] Few-shot examples of similar UIs plus coordinate overlays raised critique
  quality 55% over zero-shot (0.31 to 0.48), humans 0.75. Table 7 ^f3
  > | Zero-shot | 0.31 | 29 | 2.6 |
  > | Human | 0.75 | 17 | 1.3 |
- [web/qa] Asking for critique and locations in one call lowered critique quality; split
  into two calls. 5.2.5 ^f4
  > while the bounding boxes improved in accuracy with visual prompting, the design critiques decreased in quality. This is probably due to task overload on the LLM.
- [web/qa] Prompts gave the model explicit guideline sets and asked for violations.
  5.1 ^f5
  > We also provide the three sets guidelines that were used during the data collection and ask the LLM to find guideline violations to supplement its feedback.
- [web/qa] (inferred) For meiolol's agents: screenshot review is useful for catching
  issues but needs a written checklist and examples of good and bad pages; one narrow
  question per call beats "review this page"; it does not replace deterministic checks. ^f6
