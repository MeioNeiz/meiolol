# How to Draft Better: Team Comps, Synergies, and Counterpicks (Boostroom, 2026)

Why: how coaching content explains solo queue draft, and which named ideas (win
condition, engage vs disengage, lane priority, blind vs counter pick) a Learn page
could have players discover.

## Assessment

- Evidence: C. Commercial blog, no byline or data; matches common coaching advice (and
  the Guild Order guide) but nothing measured.
- Reasoning: fair. Gives the why for each rule and a solo queue framing (comfort before
  counters) rather than pro draft.
- Interests: Boostroom sells boosting and coaching, a service Riot's terms forbid; it
  wants players to feel draft is learnable and hard.
- Weigh: use as the vocabulary and the order of concepts coaches teach; check any
  "this beats that" against our own win rates before showing it.

## Findings

- [draft] Solo queue framing: aim for a stable, playable draft, not a pro-level one. ^f1
  > don’t try to build a perfect pro-level draft in Solo Queue. Build a stable, playable draft that gives your team a clear path to win.
- [draft] Priority order for solo queue: comfort, then structure, then counters. ^f2
  > Comfort first, structure second, counters third.
- [draft] Win condition is named as the central concept; once named, the rest follows. ^f3
  > If you can name your comp’s win condition, your draft decisions get 10x easier.
- [draft] Win condition defined as the simplest way the comp wins, with six types. ^f4
  > Win condition: the simplest way your comp wins (front-to-back, dive, pick, poke, wombo, split push).
- [draft] Engage vs disengage defined as starting vs stopping a fight. ^f5
  > Disengage: tools that stop or reset a fight (knockbacks, slows, zoning, peel).
- [draft] Lane priority defined. ^f6
  > Lane priority (prio): who can move first because their wave is pushed or they’re stronger in lane.
- [draft] [learning] Teaches draft as a fixed checklist: role job, missing pieces (engage, frontline, damage), win condition, fitting picks, purposeful bans. ^f7
  > By the time 2–3 picks are shown, check:
- [draft] Blind first pick: safe and flexible over greedy. ^f8
  > First pick should be safe and flexible, not greedy and fragile.
- [draft] Red side: take comfort blinds early, save the last pick for a real counter. ^f9
  > Don’t waste last pick on a “comfort blind” if you can take that earlier and save last pick for a real counter.
- [draft] A counter that only works if you win lane hard is a gamble. ^f10
  > If your counterpick only works when you hard-win lane and you’re not confident, it’s not a counterpick—it’s a gamble.
