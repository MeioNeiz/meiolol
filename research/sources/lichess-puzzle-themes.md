# Lichess Puzzle Themes page

Why: how a puzzle product names the concepts it teaches and lets players drill one.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Live page of the most used free puzzle site, counts per theme shown.
- Reasoning: fair. A menu, not an argument.
- Interests: none known.
- Weigh: shows structure, not whether theme drilling helps.

## Findings

- [learning] [ux/layout] The default is a mix of everything, so the player must spot the
  idea, as in a real game; themes are the opt-in drill. ^f1
  > A bit of everything. You don't know what to expect, so be ready for anything! Just like in real games.
- [learning] [ux/features] Each theme is a named idea with a one-line definition, the
  vocabulary the puzzles teach (fork, pin, skewer...). ^f2
  > A move where a piece attacks two or more opposing pieces simultaneously.
  > A tactic involving pins, where a piece is unable to move without revealing an attack on a higher value piece.
- [learning] Themes are cut several ways: phase, motif, goal (equality, advantage,
  crushing, by engine eval bands), length and origin. ^f3
  > Seize your chance to get a decisive advantage. (200cp ≤ eval ≤ 600cp)
  > Come back from a losing position, and secure a draw or a balanced position. (eval ≤ 200cp)
- [learning] Length is a difficulty axis of its own: one move up to four or more. ^f4
  > A puzzle that is only one move long.
  > Four moves or more to win.
- [learning] [ux/features] A dashboard shows the player's improvement areas and strengths,
  and puzzles can come from the player's own games (menu links Improvement areas,
  Strengths, From my games). ^f5
- [learning] Puzzles can be filtered by opening and by origin, such as games of titled
  players. ^f6
  > Puzzles from games played by titled players.
- [learning] (inferred) For meio.lol: draft themes could be named ideas (counter pick,
  blind pick safety, comp damage mix, flex pick) with a one-line definition each, a mixed
  default round and a per-theme drill fed by a weak-areas view. ^f7
