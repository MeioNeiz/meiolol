# GOV.UK writing guidelines: Write clear titles

Why: a numeric title limit and how it is justified.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. GOV.UK publishing standard, enforced by publishing tools (duplicate
  title warning in Whitehall Publisher).
- Reasoning: fair. Two reasons for 65: search cut-off and comprehension.
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: the number comes from Google's cut-off, so it is a page title limit, not a
  rule for in-page headings.

## Findings

- [ux] Titles 65 characters or less, including spaces; longer only when essential. Keep your title short ^f1
  > Your title should be 65 characters or less (including spaces).
- [ux] Reasons: Google truncates at about 65, and longer titles are harder to understand. Keep your title short ^f2
  > longer titles are harder to understand
- [ux] A title must make sense on its own ("Regulations" alone says little). Check your title makes sense ^f3
  > by itself – for example ‘Regulations’ does not say much, but ‘Regulations for environmental waste’ does
- [web/qa] One rule is tool-enforced: Whitehall Publisher warns on a duplicate title. Duplicate titles ^f4
  > This title is already used on GOV.UK. Please create a unique title
- [ux] (inferred) Checkable for meio.lol: page titles at most 65 characters and unique
  across the site, a test over the generated pages. ^f5
