# Edward Tufte: Sparkline theory and practice

Why: the original case for word-sized charts inside tables, for matchup rows with lane
curves.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Tufte coined the term; the chapter is the canonical reference.
- Reasoning: strong. Worked examples (glucose, finance, sport), costs discussed in the
  thread.
- Interests: sells his books.
- Weigh: aesthetic rules from print; check legibility at phone widths ourselves.

## Findings

- [ux/charts] Definition: a small, intense, word-sized graphic. ^f1
  > A sparkline is a small intense, simple, word-sized graphic with typographic resolution.
- [ux/density] Sparklines belong wherever a number can go: in a sentence, a table cell,
  a headline. ^f2
  > sparkline graphics can be everywhere a word or number can be: embedded in a sentence, table, headline, map, spreadsheet, graphic
- [ux/charts] A grey band for the normal range gives the line its context: deviations
  outside the band are what the eye catches. ^f3
  > Some useful context is provided by showing the _normal range_ of glucose, here as a gray band.
  > the task is to detect quickly and assess wayward deviations from normal limits, shown here by visual deviations outside the gray band
- [ux/charts] A red dot marks the one value the text refers to (the latest or the
  extreme), tying the line to a number beside it. ^f4
  > The red dot should refer to a specific number
- [ux/density] Stacked sparklines are small multiples: many variables at once with the
  same scale and form. ^f5
  > Four variables and 18,000 numbers are depicted in these small multiples.
- [ux/colour] Suggested in the thread: red and blue for above and below a zero line,
  with the grey band for normal. ^f6
  > the use of red and blue for temperatures above and below zero
