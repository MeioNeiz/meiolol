# Armasot: The Complete Guide to League Data Websites (Jun 2026)

Why: how a careful player reads rank, patch and sample filters that the big tools leave
to the user.

## Assessment

- Evidence: C. One creator (1,070 subscribers, 4,900 views); auto captions.
- Reasoning: fair. Gives reasons and examples (Smolder nerf, Caitlyn surrenders).
- Interests: none known; favours LoLalytics openly.
- Weigh: a user's view of what the tools leave undone, not evidence about any one tool.

## Findings

- [needs] [ux/uncertainty] The misuse he sees most: day-one patch data at Diamond+ or Master+ read as proof a champion is broken. @0:00 ^f1
  > Someone opens a data website on day one of a patch, clicks diamond plus or master plus, looks at few numbers, and then suddenly that champion is broken.
- [stats] [ux/controls] He picks 30 days at Emerald+, or Gold+ for rarer items, for sample over rank. @0:52 ^f2
  > In most situations, I use a 30 days data and emerald plus
- [stats] Master+ data answers a skill-ceiling question; for how good a champion is, more games matter more. @1:40 ^f3
  > for that, having a larger sample size is usually far more valuable.
- [method/dynamics] After a big change a 30-day window is useless, since most games predate it: the patch-day trade-off left to the user. @1:40 ^f4
  > Using 30 days data would be pointless because most of those games happened before the nerfs.
- [stats] [ux/uncertainty] Judge a sample by its game count, not its pick rate: 0.1% pick can still be 10,000 games. @6:58 ^f5
  > Imagine an item has a 0.1% pick rate but it has still represents 10,000 games.
- [stats] His own comfort level: 10,000 games, often 2,000 to 5,000 for clear trends. @7:45 ^f6
  > Even two to five K games can often reveal clear trends.
