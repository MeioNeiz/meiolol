# Don't counterpick with a champion you have no experience on, especially in low elo (r/summonerschool, Aug 2020)

Why: do players distrust tools that "just tell you what to pick", and why?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. 3.0k upvotes, 97% upvoted, 273 comments. 2020, but the advice is evergreen
  on r/summonerschool.
- Reasoning: fair. Players argue with numbers (2-4% matchup gaps) and anecdotes.
- Interests: none; many commenters are mains of the champions discussed.
- Weigh: community consensus, not measurement. Directly relevant to meiolol's own pick
  recommender: comfort and experience outweigh counter stats at low elo.

## Findings

- [draft] [needs] Consensus: in low elo a counter pick on an unfamiliar champion is not a
  counter; stick to what you know. ^f1
  > As a rule of thumb especially in low elo, a counterpick isnt really a counterpick if you never play the champion.
- [market] Reception: 3.0k upvotes, 97% upvoted. ^f2
  > Don't try to counterpick someone by playing a champion you have no experience on, especially in low elo.
- [draft] [tools] Counter pages are blamed by name: players first-time a champion because
  a site says it counters. ^f3
  > Don't just pick a random champion you have 0 experience with because "according to this site it counters the enemy laner".
- [draft] [learning] [needs] Stats sites say a pick is favoured but not how to exploit
  it; the gap players feel is the how. ^f4
  > Well the stats and builds are accurate, the thing is that they don't explain how to actually exploit your advantage.
  > If you don't know why a pick is a counterpick, it isn't a counterpick.
- [draft] [method] Counter gaps are small (2-4% win rate) and global, so personal
  win rates per champion and matchup matter more. ^f5
  > The "counter" picks are normally only 2-4% difference in win rate vs each other.
  > The 4% difference comes from a global statistics. It may not apply to you. Your own win rates with different champions and match-ups is going to be different.
- [learning] [needs] Players learn matchups by stitching sources: OP.GG counter page and
  lane kill rate, then googling "X vs Y reddit" for both mains subreddits' tips. ^f6
  > I look at winrates and lane kill potential at OP.GG and then look for tips on the matchup on the X champion mains subreddits thanks to a google search.
- [learning] Alternative advice: learn the enemy champion's weakness and apply it with
  champions you already play. ^f7
  > Learn what that champ's weakness is, and apply it to the champs you know how to play.
- [draft] Players check opponents' experience on OP.GG or Porofessor to spot first-timed
  counter picks. ^f8
  > I check OP.GG/Porofessor -> No previous experience on the champs.
