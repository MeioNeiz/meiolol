# Reuters Institute: Moving the needle (2024)

Why: how the NYT needle handles thin data eight years on: when it is shown at all,
and what it starts from.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Interview with the editor running NYT election results since 2016.
- Reasoning: fair. Says what and some why; no numbers on the model.
- Interests: the NYT describing its own product, via a journalism institute.
- Weigh: trust as self-description; nothing on the needle's look.

## Findings

- [ux/uncertainty] With few votes in, the needle shows the pre-election estimate and
  moves only as counted votes arrive: a prior that thin data barely shifts. ^f1
  > "if turned on before enough votes are counted in a place, the needle’s forecast would be based on the pre-election estimate"
- [ux/uncertainty] [ux/trust] A data floor for the whole widget: no needle is run for
  a state unless its data are detailed enough; 2020 had needles for only three states. ^f2
  > "We will only publish the forecast when we are confident that it can be a reliable source of information for readers."
  > "only ran needles for three states"
- [ux/trust] The stated job of the needle is to tell readers to discount early raw
  counts that may skew one way. ^f3
  > "you should probably take that with a grain of salt, because we know that the reported vote might skew one way or the other"
- [ux/trust] It never calls a race; calls come from the AP. ^f4
  > "we don’t use the needle as an explicit race-calling tool"
