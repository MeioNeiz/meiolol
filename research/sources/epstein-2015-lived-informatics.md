# Epstein et al. 2015: A Lived Informatics Model of Personal Informatics (UbiComp)

Why: a player checking their own games is self-tracking with automatic collection; what
does the self-tracking literature say about review rhythm and dropping off?

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Peer-reviewed (UbiComp 2015), the standard model in personal informatics
  research; surveys of 105, 99 and 83 trackers plus 22 interviews.
- Reasoning: fair. Descriptive, US Mechanical Turk sample, three domains (activity,
  finance, location), not games.
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: games differ (no manual entry); trust the lapse categories and review-rhythm
  shape, not the percentages.

## Findings

- [retention] People lapse in four ways: forgetting, upkeep, skipping entries,
  suspending. ^f1
  > forgetting to track, difficulty managing upkeep,
  > intentionally skipping entries, and suspending tracking.
- [retention] Many returning trackers need a nudge; forgetting several times turns into
  quitting. ^f2
  > people may decide that tracking is not worth the trouble after
  > “I just didn’t make it a habit and then kind of forgot about it.”
- [retention] Review rhythm depends on domain: activity most often, finance weekly
  (35%), location monthly or less; few review daily. ^f3
  > Financial trackers reported reviewing weekly (35% and 33%
  > reported reviewing their data every day for the past 7 days).
- [retention] Aggregation over a week or month can be enough to get value from slower
  data. ^f4
  > while aggregation across a week or month may be
- [retention] Open design problem named: helping people resume after a lapse, using the
  history from before (or during) the lapse. ^f5
  > Designing to resume tracking after a lapse, including how
- [retention] Some track for self-understanding and never act on the data. ^f6
  > for self-understanding [34], never acting upon their data.
- [retention] (inferred) For meio.lol: the Riot API removes forgetting and upkeep (games
  are collected whether or not the player visits), so a return can always open on "since
  you were last here"; a weekly digest fits players who review weekly, not daily. ^f7
