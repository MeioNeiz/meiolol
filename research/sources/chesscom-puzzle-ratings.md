# Chess.com Help: How do Puzzle ratings work?

Why: how a puzzle product sets difficulty and keeps puzzles solvable.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Vendor help, August 2026, describing a system used by millions.
- Reasoning: fair. Explains the trade between gains and losses per setting.
- Interests: vendor.
- Weigh: the redesign towards easier Standard puzzles is a product choice, not data.

## Findings

- [learning] Default puzzles were made easier: most players should solve most of them;
  harder settings are opt-in. ^f1
  > Puzzles are designed to be challenging but solvable, and most players should be able to solve the majority of them.
  > Standard puzzles are generally easier than those in the previous Puzzle system while still providing a meaningful challenge.
- [learning] A hint counts as a failed attempt for rating. ^f2
  > Using a Hint counts as a failed attempt, just like making an incorrect move.
- [learning] A new puzzle's rating comes from who solves it, then is locked. ^f3
  > When a puzzle is first added, its rating is determined by who is able to solve it. After a set period, the puzzle’s rating becomes locked and does not change further.
- [learning] (inferred) Chess.com and Lichess agree on the core: puzzle difficulty is measured from
  attempts on one scale with the player (compare [[lichess-puzzle-db#^f2]]). ^f4
