# Chen, Jiang, Noble, Lu 2024: Diamond

Why: how to put a false discovery rate on pairs a trained net claims, so a synergy or
counter table from a black box is not just its top-ranked noise.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. NeurIPS 2024 (earlier preprint DeepROCK); Noble is an established statistical
  genomics author; simulations on the NID suite (20k samples, 30 features, 20 repeats)
  and small real sets (442 diabetes patients). No large noisy categorical data.
- Reasoning: fair. Shows the failure mode it fixes (naive scores break FDR), tests
  robustness to knockoff method and importance measure; distillation is described
  only briefly in the results.
- Interests: authors' method.
- Weigh: the result that matters to us is that even permuted copies as negative controls
  kept FDR, with less power; the exact FDR is for continuous features, so our
  categorical seats would need checking with planted pairs.

## Findings

- [method/interpret] [stats] Model-X knockoffs: dummy copies of each feature that mimic
  the features' joint distribution but carry no signal given the originals; pairs among
  originals are compared with pairs involving knockoffs to estimate FDR. Section 2.1 ^f1
  > The core idea of knockoffs is to generate dummy features that perfectly mimic the empirical dependence structure among the original features but are conditionally independent of the response given the original features.
- [method/interpret] Off-the-shelf interaction scores mostly reflect two individually
  important features, not their non-additive effect; used as is they break FDR control.
  Section 1, 2.2 ^f2
  > these approaches characterize feature pairs where both features are individually important for a model’s prediction, rather than capturing the synergistic or interaction effects between the two features
  > without it, the FDR cannot be controlled by naively using reported interaction importance values from existing methods
- [stats] Permutation p-values per pair with Benjamini-Hochberg or Benjamini-Yekutieli,
  and pair FDR built from feature FDR, all failed to control the FDR. Section 2.2 ^f3
  > Our analysis reveals that none of these baseline methods effectively control the FDR.
- [stats] Even invalid knockoffs, each feature permuted independently across samples,
  kept FDR control, at a loss of power. Section 2.3 ^f4
  > Diamond maintains FDR control when paired with invalid knockoffs, generated by independently permuting each feature across samples, albeit at the cost of reduced power.
- [method/interpret] Robust across knockoff generators and importance measures (expected
  Hessian, integrated Hessians, weight-based); conservative, overestimating FDR. Section 2.3 ^f5
  > Diamond tends to be conservative by overestimating the FDR
- [method/interpret] Works with MLPs, FT-Transformer, KANs, GBDTs and factorisation
  machines; MLP and FT-Transformer had the most power, helped by a pairwise-coupling
  input layer. Section 2.2 ^f6
  > This superior performance is attributed to the inclusion of a knockoff-tailored plugin pairwise-coupling layer design
- [stats] Simulation setting: n = 20,000, p = 30 continuous uniform features, 10 of them
  active, target FDR 0.2, 20 repeats. Section 2.2 ^f7
  > we employed a sample size of n = 20, 000, equally divided into training and test sets.
- [method/interpret] Checked, not there: categorical or set-structured inputs, noisy
  near-chance binary labels, 3-way interactions (higher-order truths split into pairs). ^f8
- [method/interpret] (inferred) For us: add knockoff seats to the net (a second,
  dummy champion per seat drawn from that role's pick distribution, independent of the
  game, ^f4's permuted version), train, compute every champion-pair score for real and
  for knockoff-involving pairs, subtract both members' main contributions, and set the
  cut-off where the knockoff pairs' share reaches 20%. That is a calibrated version of
  our shuffled-profile control, at pair rather than model level. ^f9
