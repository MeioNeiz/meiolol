# Chess.com Help: What do the colored arrows in Game Review mean?

Why: how the board illustrates the Coach's sentence.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Vendor help page, updated late Oct 2026.
- Reasoning: fair.
- Interests: vendor.
- Weigh: a small, concrete colour code; on-screen detail.

## Findings

- [ux/postgame] The Coach draws arrows and squares that illustrate the bubble's text,
  several per move. ^f1
  > your Coach may display colored arrows and highlighted squares on the board to help illustrate the feedback shown in the speech bubble.
- [ux/colour] Four colours by meaning: green a better move, red a threat or problem,
  orange the main idea, blue the opponent's plan. ^f2
  > A stronger move you could have played or another good option your Coach wants to highlight.
  > A threat, tactical danger, or problem in the position, such as an opponent's threat or a mistake that loses material.
  > Your opponent's plans or possible responses when your Coach explains the position from their perspective.
- [ux/postgame] Separately, a green best-move arrow appears when the played move was not
  best (setting dependent). ^f3
  > you may also see a separate green arrow indicating the engine's best move when your played move wasn't the best.
