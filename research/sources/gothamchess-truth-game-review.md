# GothamChess: The Truth About Game Review

Why: what the web Game Review screens look like in use, and how players react (comments
with like counts).

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. First-hand use by the biggest chess YouTuber; 2,122,499 views, 61,195
  likes (6 Nov 2026). UI is June 2023 web; the 2026 help pages
  ([[chesscom-game-review-help]]) show the same flow with Skills and colour arrows added.
- Reasoning: fair. Shows where labels mislead (practical vs engine-perfect), not why the
  design is so.
- Interests: Rozman is a long-time chess.com partner and sells his own courses
  (Chessly, in the description): he has reasons both to praise review and to say it is
  not enough.
- Weigh: frames are reliable for layout; comments are reception (likes are a signal of
  shared feeling, not evidence of use).

## Findings

- [ux/postgame] Summary screen (web, 2023), top to bottom: eval graph strip, Game Review
  title, both players' Accuracy side by side (77.9 vs 81.5) with the result between,
  then a two-column count table per label for each player (Brilliant, Great Move, Best
  Move, Excellent, Good, Book, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Miss, Blunder, each with its icon
  colour), a green Start Review button, a one-line Coach verdict ("Wild: A wild game,
  and you came out on top!"), Reviewing as Black and a Show Best Moves toggle.
  @8:46, frames/8-46.jpg ^f1
- [ux/postgame] The last book move bubble: book icon, "d5 is the last book move", eval
  +0.60 in a box, one line of idea ("This prepares your bishop for development"), the
  opening name, "You've played this opening 460 times, with a 60% win rate", a
  won/drawn/lost bar (278/13/169), master game moves with result bars, Explore, and a
  green Next Key Move button. @9:46, frames/9-46.jpg ^f2
- [ux/postgame] Per move screen: the eval graph stays on top with a marker at the
  current move; the bubble is the label icon and "Nf3 is excellent", the eval in a box,
  a short sentence ("That's a sensible reply."), Show Moves; under it Retry, Best and a
  green Next; label icons also sit beside moves in the move list. @19:47,
  frames/19-47.jpg ^f3
- [ux/postgame] On the board, the label icon (e.g. ?! for inaccuracy) sits on the moved
  piece's square, and an arrow shows the engine's preferred move. @12:43,
  frames/12-43.jpg ^f4
- [ux/postgame] [learning] The Coach text names the missed idea in one line, which he
  reads aloud and then has to explain himself. @11:12 ^f5
  > game review also gives my opponents move C3 about this overlooks an opportunity to develop a night off at starting Square this permits the opponent to kick a bishop
- [learning] His critique: the coach says what was missed, not why; you must work out
  the reason from the engine line. @21:01 ^f6
  > when game review tells you you miss a chance to attack a bishop it's not telling you you you're gonna get the bishop sometimes
  > it's important to understand what exactly was wrong with the move yes you can hit best and yes you can hit show moves but toggle back do a little bit of a deep dive
- [ux/trust] Labels are engine-perfect, not practical: a move is a Mistake only if the
  opponent finds one exact reply. @15:58 ^f7
  > D3 is a bad move if and only if the move C4 and Knight C3 get played according to the eval but that's it only if that
- [ux/trust] Review ignores the clock: low-time moves are judged as if there was time.
  @19:31 ^f8
  > it's still analyzing the moves themselves it's not actually analyzing the fact that there was two seconds one day it might be smart enough to be like well yeah low time so it's not a big deal
- [ux/trust] Game Review and the browser engine can disagree because they run at
  different depths (20 vs 22). @17:28 ^f9
  > game review might run at a different depth my browser stockfish goes to a 22 depth and this is a 20 depth
- [needs] Top comment (16,000 likes): players trust review when it flatters them. ^f10
  > Personally, I find that the game review feature is accurate when it says I played well and inaccurate when it says I played poorly.
