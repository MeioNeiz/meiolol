# ICO guide to lawful basis: Legitimate interests

Why: the likely lawful basis for storing and showing public match data of players who
have not signed up; what the UK regulator expects a solo developer to do.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. The UK regulator's own guidance, updated 23 March 2026 (post Data (Use and
  Access) Act; mentions the new "recognised legitimate interest" basis).
- Reasoning: strong. Explains conditions, tests and when not to use the basis.
- Interests: regulator.
- Weigh: UK GDPR. EU GDPR Art 6(1)(f) is the same text, so the test carries over to EU
  players, but EU regulators (e.g. CNIL) interpret independently.

## Findings

- [privacy] Legitimate interests fits use people would reasonably expect, with minimal
  privacy impact, or a compelling justification. ^f1
  > in ways they would reasonably expect;
  > in ways that have a minimal impact on their privacy; or
- [privacy] Three-part test: identify the interest, show necessity, balance against the
  person's rights. ^f2
  > identify a legitimate interest;
  > show that the use of personal information is necessary to achieve it; and
  > balance it against the interests, rights and freedoms of the person whose information you want to use.
- [privacy] Commercial and individual interests count. ^f3
  > commercial interests;
- [privacy] Necessity: not available if a less intrusive way achieves the same result. ^f4
  > If you can reasonably achieve the same result in another less intrusive way, legitimate interests doesn’t apply.
- [privacy] The basis must be stated in the privacy information; an LIA should be
  recorded. ^f5
  > You **should** keep a record of your legitimate interests assessment (LIA) to help you demonstrate compliance if needed.
  > You **must** include details of your legitimate interests in your privacy information.
- [privacy] Right to object applies; outside direct marketing you must stop unless you
  show compelling grounds. Portability does not apply. ^f6
  > For other purposes, you **must** stop unless you can show that you have compelling legitimate grounds to override that person’s rights.
  > But the right to data portability doesn’t apply.
- [privacy] Checklist: consider making objection easy, such as an opt-out. ^f7
  > We've considered how we can make it easy for people to object (such as whether we can offer an opt-out).
- [privacy] Take extra care with children's data (relevant: League players include
  teenagers). ^f8
  > Legitimate interests can apply if you intend to use children’s information. But you **must** take extra care to protect their interests.
- [privacy] (inferred) For meiolol: showing a looked-up player's own games against public
  curves plausibly passes (expected use, low impact, as op.gg-style sites have trained
  players to expect); a per-player opt-out honouring objections is the cheap safeguard
  ICO points to. Squads (named groups) is a different purpose and fits consent better. ^f9
