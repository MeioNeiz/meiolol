# Learning to Warm Up Cold Item Embeddings for Cold-start Recommendation with Meta Scaling and Shifting Networks

Why: the main follow-up to Meta-Embedding; tests a trivially simple init (mean
embedding) against learnt generators and adds a correction of a few-sample embedding
toward a stable target, close to what a patched champion needs.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. SIGIR 2021, three public datasets, ten runs with a paired t-test,
  comparisons with DropoutNet, MeLU, MetaEmb and sequential models; offline only; AUC
  only (no log loss), so calibration is unknown.
- Reasoning: fair. Ablation per component; explanations of mixed results are
  speculative.
- Interests: authors' own method.
- Weigh: trust the ablation's direction (mean init beats random; most of the edge fades
  as data arrives); the meta-net gains are AUC on small benchmarks.

## Findings

- [method/dynamics] Two problems with a new item's embedding: it doesn't fit the deep
  model trained mostly on hot items, and a few noisy samples move it a lot. 1 ^f1
  > A gap is existing between the cold ID embedding and the deep model.
  > even small noise can have a serious negative impact on the cold ID embedding generated from limited samples
- [method/dynamics] Common initial embedding: every new item starts from the average
  embedding of all existing items, not random. 3.2 ^f2
  > it is intuitive to take the average embeddings of all existing items to initialize ID embeddings of new items.
- [method/dynamics] Scaling net (from item features) maps the cold embedding toward the
  warm space; shifting net (from the mean embedding of users who interacted) adds a
  stable offset: v_warm = v * tau_scale + tau_shift. 3.3 ^f3
  > The scaling function can be seen as a kind of feature transformation, which transforms the cold ID embedding into the warm feature space.
- [method/dynamics] The base model and old embeddings stay fixed while the meta nets
  train, so hot items are untouched; only cold items pass through MWUF. 1, 3.4 ^f4
  > To avoid disturbing the recommendation of old items, we leave the parameters of 𝜃 and 𝜙 fixed.
  > only cold items need to pass the MWUF, which means the MWUF has no influence on the hot items.
- [method/dynamics] Protocol (after Meta-Embedding): new items' samples sorted by time;
  the first 3K form warm-a, -b, -c, the rest is the test set; each phase is scored
  before the embeddings train on it; one epoch per step; ten runs. 4.1 ^f5
  > The samples of each new item are sorted by timestamp, and we use the first 3 × 𝐾 as 3 warm-up phases denoted as warm-a, -b, -c while the rest as a test set.
  > Update the embeddings of new items IDs with warm-a set and compute evaluation metrics on the warm-b set
- [method/dynamics] Cold-start methods' advantage shrinks as an item's data grows: the
  gap between cold embedding and model closes with more samples. 4.3 ^f6
  > the improvement (RelaImpr) of cold-start methods decreases in later stages.
- [method/dynamics] Mean-embedding init alone beats random init in both phases and is
  about as good as MetaEmb's learnt generator in the cold phase; MetaEmb is better in
  warm-up. 4.5, 4.6 ^f7
  > MWUF(init) outperforms Wide & Deep, which demonstrates the common initialization is help for both the cold-start and warm-up phases.
  > in the cold-start phase, MUWF(init) can obtain comparable results with MetaEmb.
- [method/dynamics] Scaling (feature-driven transform) contributes more than shifting
  (neighbour average) in the ablation. 4.5 ^f8
  > MWUF(scale) achieves better performance than MWUF(shift), which demonstrates that Meta Scaling Network is more effective than Meta Shifting Network.
- [method/dynamics] Sequential models that use the item's few interactions directly do
  badly cold but very well in warm-up, sometimes matching the cold-start methods. 4.3 ^f9
  > by effectively exploiting the limited interactions, these models achieve remarkable performance on the warm-up phases
- [method/dynamics] Checked, not there: only new items; no handling of an existing item
  whose behaviour shifts; no log loss. ^f10
- [method/dynamics] (inferred) For us: a patched champion's embedding is "noisy and
  shifted" after a few hundred new-patch games, the case MWUF's scale/shift targets;
  the cheap analogue is a per-champion-role offset on the new patch, shrunk to zero,
  with the frozen trunk. A brand-new champion should start at the mean embedding for
  its role, not random. Score each warm-up slice before training on it. ^f11
