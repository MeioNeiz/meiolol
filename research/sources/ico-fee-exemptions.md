# ICO data protection fee exemptions

Why: could meiolol fall under an exemption (not-for-profit, personal, household)?

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. Regulator's page.
- Reasoning: fair.
- Interests: regulator.
- Weigh: exemptions apply only when processing is solely for listed purposes.

## Findings

- [privacy] [policy] Exempt only if processing is solely for listed purposes: staff
  admin, advertising and PR, accounts, not-for-profit, personal or household affairs,
  public register, judicial, non-automated. ^f1
  > You don’t need to pay a fee if you are processing personal data **only** for one (or more) of the following purposes:
  > Not-for-profit purposes.
  > Personal, family or household affairs.
- [privacy] Exempt controllers still have all other data protection duties. ^f2
  > even if you are exempt from paying a fee, you still need to comply with your other data protection obligations.
- [privacy] (inferred) A public site storing and showing players' match histories is not
  personal or household use; "not-for-profit purposes" in the regulations covers
  not-for-profit bodies, so a hobby site with ads likely pays tier 1 (£52,
  [[ico-data-protection-fee#^f2]]). Verify with the self-assessment tool. ^f3
