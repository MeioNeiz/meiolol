# Gorishniy et al.: TabM

Why: the current best-known recipe for a plain tabular net, with its tuned hyperparameter
space, and a cheap way to get an ensemble for log loss.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. ICLR 2025, Yandex tabular group (FT-Transformer authors), code public,
  46 datasets, 15 seeds per config; also scaled best in [[actuarial-scaling-2026#^f4]],
  an independent noisy-target study.
- Reasoning: strong. Ablations of each design step, analysis of why it works and of k.
- Interests: authors propose TabM.
- Weigh: trust the protocol and tuning spaces; their datasets are mostly numerical,
  not 10 high-cardinality categoricals.

## Findings

- [method] TabM: one MLP imitating an ensemble of k MLPs that are trained together and
  share most weights; MLP-based models beat attention- and retrieval-based ones and TabM
  was best overall. Abstract ^f1
  > Generally, we show that MLPs, including TabM, form a line of stronger and more practical models compared to attention- and retrievalbased architectures.
  > In particular, we find that TabM demonstrates the best performance among tabular DL models.
- [method] The only new hyperparameter is k, fixed at 32 untuned. 3.3 ^f2
  > We heuristically set k = 32 and do not tune this value.
- [method] TabM-mini keeps only one non-shared multiplicative adapter on the input of
  the first layer, and does slightly better than the naive full version. 3.3 ^f3
  > we keep only the very first adapter of TabMnaive and remove the remaining 3N − 1 adapters, which gives us TabMmini — the minimal version of TabM.
- [method] Training optimises the mean of the k individual losses; inference uses the
  mean prediction. 5.1 ^f4
  > the mean of the k individual losses is what is explicitly optimized during the training of TabMmini , the loss of the collective mean prediction corresponds to how TabMmini makes predictions on inference
- [method] Submodels look overfitted individually yet the mean generalises much better;
  even the best single submodel is no better than a plain MLP. 5.1 ^f5
  > Compared to the baseline MLP, the submodels look overfitted individually, while their collective prediction exhibits substantially better generalization.
  > Summary. TabM draws its power from the collective prediction of weak, but diverse submodels.
- [method] Too large k hurts, and too narrow (d = 64) or too shallow (1 layer) TabM is
  suboptimal on middle-to-large data. 5.3 ^f6
  > too high values of k can be detrimental.
  > too narrow (d = 64) or too shallow (n = 1) configurations of TabM can lead to suboptimal performance
- [method] Optimal learning rate for TabM is higher than for MLP at the same batch size.
  A.3 ^f7
  > We noticed that the typical optimal learning rate for TabM is higher than for MLP
- [method] Protocol: AdamW, no learning rate schedule, no augmentation, gradient
  clipping at 1.0, dataset-specific batch size, patience 16 epochs, Optuna TPE 50-100
  iterations; categoricals one-hot. D.1 ^f8
  > We use the AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019). We do not apply learning rate schedules. We do not use data augmentations. We apply global gradient clipping to 1.0.
  > we set patience = 16 for the DL models.
  > For categorical features, we used one-hot encoding.
- [method] TabM tuning space: k = 32, 1-5 layers, width 64-1024, dropout {0, U[0, 0.5]},
  lr LogUniform[1e-4, 5e-3], weight decay {0, LogUniform[1e-4, 1e-1]}. Table 6 ^f9
  > UniformInt[1, 5]
  > LogUniform[1e-4, 5e-3]
- [method] MLP tuning space: 1-6 layers, width 64-1024, dropout {0, U[0, 0.5]}, lr
  LogUniform[3e-5, 1e-3], weight decay {0, LogUniform[1e-4, 1e-1]}, 100 iterations.
  Table 8 ^f10
  > Table 8: The hyperparameter tuning space for MLP.
  > LogUniform[3e-5, 1e-3]
- [method] (inferred) For us, TabM-mini on top of a rating offset gives a 32-way
  ensemble in one training run, which is the cheap route to the convexity gain in log
  loss ([[actuarial-scaling-2026#^f5]]). ^f11
