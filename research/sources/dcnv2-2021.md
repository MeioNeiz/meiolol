# DCN-V2 (Google, WWW 2021)

Why: DCN-V2 is the explicit-cross net most used in production CTR; does an explicit
cross layer beat a tuned MLP, and by how much, at tens of millions of rows?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Google, deployed in several ranking systems, 5 runs per setting with
  standard deviations; public Criteo and MovieLens plus production data.
- Reasoning: strong. Tunes the DNN baseline as hard as its own model, and reports that
  most published crossing models do not beat it.
- Interests: authors propose DCN-V2.
- Weigh: random splits, CTR data; the ~0.0015 nats gain is on a far stronger
  interaction signal than drafts.

## Findings

- [method/tabular] Tuned with a larger DNN than the literature used, a plain DNN ran neck
  and neck with most crossing baselines and beat some. 7.3 ^f1
  > DNNs are universal approximators and are tough-to-beat baselines when highlyoptimized.
  > To our surprise, DNN performed neck to neck with most baselines and even outperformed certain models.
- [method/tabular] Explicit crosses that are hard to optimise lose to a big DNN, which
  dominates the combined model. 7.3 ^f2
  > Hence, when integrated with DNN, the overall performance is dominated by the DNN component.
- [method/tabular] Criteo test log loss, 5 runs: PNN, DeepFM, xDeepFM, AutoInt+, DCN and
  DNN all 0.4420-0.4421 (DLRM 0.4427); DCN-V2 0.4406, DCN-Mix 0.4408, CrossNet alone 0.4413.
  Table 6 ^f3
  > 0.4421 (6.5E-5)
  > 0.4406 (6.2E-5)
- [method/tabular] 0.001 is called significant on Criteo; DCN-V2 was best by modelling up
  to third-order crosses beyond the DNN's implicit ones. 7.2 ^f4
  > DCN-V2 achieved the best performance (0.001 considered to be significant on Criteo [26, 46, 50]) by explicitly modeling up to 3rdorder crosses beyond those implicit ones from DNN.
- [method/tabular] Higher-order crosses add less than lower-order ones as layers are
  added. 7.4 ^f5
  > This suggests the contribution from that of higher-order crosses is less significant than those from lower-order crosses.
- [method/tabular] (inferred) For us: a cross layer is cheap to try inside the set net or
  big net (replace or precede the first ReLU layer), but the evidence says a tuned MLP
  already captures most crosses; expect a gain only if the draft has strong bounded-degree
  products, which our pair results do not suggest. ^f6
