# Peck, Ayuso, El-Etr (CHI 2019): Data is Personal

Why: what makes people pay attention to a data display, beyond its design.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Peer-reviewed CHI paper (best paper), qualitative: 42 semi-structured
  interviews, chart rankings coded; data on OSF.
- Reasoning: fair. Careful about generalising; quotes participants.
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: small non-gamer sample on opioid charts; direction (own context draws
  attention) transfers, the size does not.

## Findings

- [ux/features] Seeing their own place in a chart changed rankings dramatically, even
  for the least liked chart. Data is Personal ^f1
  > finding a reference to their personal context made a dramatic impact in their ranking.
- [ux/features] Participant explanation: interest because it affects them personally.
  Data is Personal ^f2
  > It has my own county in it. If this would have been like all the countries of the world or something, I really wouldn’t have been interested in it
- [ux/features] Attitudes to charts depend on education, politics and personal
  experience, not only design. Abstract ^f3
  > a complex set of factors intermix to inform attitudes and perceptions about data visualization - including educational background, political affiliation, and personal experience.
- [ux/features] (inferred) For meio.lol: lead with the visitor's own champion, rank and
  region (Your games, remembered filters) rather than a generic overview; it is the
  cheapest engagement lever and needs no extra words. ^f4
