# WaveClear research page: The Focus Challenge, April 2024

Why: what evidence stands behind WaveClear's win-rate claims, and how its training loop works.

## Assessment

- Evidence: D. Self-run, 2,047 matches, no control group, no definition of "skill" beyond
  kills, deaths and assists, players self-selected into a challenge.
- Reasoning: weak; win rates above 50% among volunteers who completed loops are read as the
  app's effect, with no baseline or regression to the mean considered.
- Interests: vendor marketing.
- Weigh: useful for how the product works (goal loops on one KDA stat); the numbers prove
  nothing.

## Findings

- [learning] [ux/features] The training loop: pick your worst skill (kills, deaths or assists),
  commit to a 3 or 5 match loop with any champion and role, and the Focus Tracker keeps you on
  it during play. ^f1
  > commit to 3 or 5 match “loops” to practice your worst skill (Kills, Deaths, Assist). While playing, the Focus Tracker keeps you focused on improving that skill.
- [learning] The experiment covered 2,047 matches and 513 completed training loops (short page
  lines, unquotable). ^f2
- [learning] (claim) Says mindset tools alone gave a 55% win rate and improving one skill 58%,
  with no control group described. ^f3
  > Improving skills or not, just using mindset-based training tools boost win rate.
  > Improving one skill almost DOUBLES your win rate increase!
- [learning] (claim) Says goals are personalised to skill level, tier, champion and role using
  goal setting theory. ^f4
  > WaveClear personalizes training to your skill level, tier, champ, and role while using the Goal Setting Theory
- [learning] (claim) Says skills improved 36% on average within 1 to 3 matches. ^f5
  > In 1-3 matches only, gamers show great improvement in key game skills
- [learning] (inferred) Short loops starting from a player's worst recent stat will show
  "improvement" through regression to the mean alone; the page does not address it. ^f6
