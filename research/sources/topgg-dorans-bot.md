# Dorans-bot on top.gg (League Discord tracker bot)

Why: what social/friend-group tools exist for League, and how big do they get?

## Assessment

- Evidence: C. Listing shows 4.67 from 9 reviews, 659 votes and "2.31K" (top.gg's server count figure); small next to stats sites. Search also showed similar bots (Loldosa ~1.1K, LP tracker) on top.gg.
- Reasoning: fair; clear feature list.
- Interests: developer's own listing.
- Weigh: shows the friend-group format is alive and multi-competitor; no player or revenue figures.

## Findings

- [fun] [tools] Discord friend-group tracker: posts every game when it ends with KDA, LP, items and "fun facts". ^f1
  > Every game posted to Discord the moment it ends. KDA, LP changes, items, damage, and fun facts.
- [fun] Group stats: auto-updating server leaderboards and daily recaps of who climbed and fell. ^f2
  > Auto-updating rankings for every linked player. See who's actually the best.
  > Daily recaps show who climbed and who fell.
- [fun] [ux] Each Discord server gets a public web page with history, profiles, leaderboards, analytics. ^f3
  > Every server gets a dedicated web page with match history, profiles, leaderboards, and analytics.
- [fun] [policy] Play-money predictions on friends' live games with a prediction leaderboard (gambling-adjacent; Riot policy on betting would matter). ^f4
  > Bet gold on your friends' live games. Will they carry or int? Climb the prediction leaderboard.
- [market] Scale: about 2.3K on top.gg's count, 659 votes, 9 reviews (numbers on short lines, unquotable). ^f5
- [fun] [market] (inferred) Friend-group Discord trackers are a crowded small-scale niche (Dorans-bot, Loldosa, LP tracker seen in one search); none is large. ^f6
