# UK GDPR Article 5, principles (legislation.gov.uk, revised to 5 Feb 2026)

Why: privacy-rules.md said "storage limitation, Art 5(1)(e); not yet quoted in research/".

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. The law itself, the UK government's revised text with every amendment
  dated (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes in force 5 Feb 2026)
- Reasoning: fair. Statute, no reasons given
- Interests: none
- Weigh: binding for a UK controller; the EU text of 5(1)(e) differs only in citing
  Article 89(1) instead of 84B

## Findings

- [privacy] Storage limitation: personal data kept identifiable no longer than the purpose needs; no period is set by law. Art 5(1)(e) ^f1
  > kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed
- [privacy] Longer storage is allowed for statistical or research purposes under Article 84B safeguards. Art 5(1)(e) ^f2
  > personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as the personal data will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes
- [privacy] Accountability: the controller must be able to show it complies (so retention lengths and sweeps should be written down). Art 5(2) ^f3
  > The controller shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate compliance with, paragraph 1
- [privacy] Data minimisation: only what the purposes need. Art 5(1)(c) ^f4
  > adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed
- [privacy] Transparency is a principle in its own right, alongside lawfulness and fairness. Art 5(1)(a) ^f5
  > processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject
