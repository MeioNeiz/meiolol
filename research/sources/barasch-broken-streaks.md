# CU Boulder Leeds news: Broken Records: When Streaks End (on Silverman and Barasch 2023, JCR)

Why: the critic's side of streaks: what happens when a streak breaks?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Peer-reviewed JCR paper with field data from a language app (streaks
  kept, broken by the user, reset by a glitch); read here through the university's
  interview with the author, not the paper.
- Reasoning: fair. Interview, mechanism stated (the record no longer represents you).
- Interests: university press office.
- Weigh: direction trusted; effect sizes not in this piece.

## Findings

- [retention] After a broken streak people often abandon the activity. ^f1
  > once those streaks are broken, people often abandon the activity.
- [retention] Messaging that points at the failure with a nudge back typically didn't
  work; people dropped off. ^f2
  > We found that kind of messaging typically doesn’t work, and that people will just drop off once their streaks are broken.
- [retention] Users switched to something new (another language) rather than restart the
  same streak: the record no longer represented them. ^f3
  > We found people would switch to a new language when their streak broke, as opposed to building a new streak with the same language
- [retention] Suggested alternatives: offer a new goal, or choose goal periods that give
  more chances without breaking the streak. ^f4
  > adjusting the time periods for goals to offer more opportunities to motivate users without breaking existing streaks
- [retention] Field data: streak kept, broken by the user, or reset by a glitch. ^f5
  > who either maintained their streak in a language learning app, broke the streak through their own behavior or had their streak reset due to a glitch
- [retention] (inferred) For meio.lol: a daily streak is a quit point when it breaks;
  weekly marks or forgiving streaks (Daily already forgives one miss) and "since you were
  last here" on return beat a "you lost your streak" message. ^f6
