# Duolingo blog: How Streaks keep Duolingo learners committed to their language goals (2017)

Why: what a daily streak does to return visits, from the product that made it famous.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. First-hand A/B results from Duolingo's growth team, but no sample sizes
  or test lengths given.
- Reasoning: fair. States hypotheses and results, little on costs.
- Interests: company blog, partly a hiring post.
- Weigh: trust direction of effects; the percentages are unaudited. Streaks drive return
  visits, not learning as such.

## Findings

- [fun] A streak counts days in a row meeting a daily goal; one missed day resets it;
  shown on the home screen with reminders. ^f1
  > Miss a single day, and your streak resets to zero.
  > We display learners’ streaks prominently throughout their Duolingo experience using a flame icon.
- [fun] Learners hate losing a streak. ^f2
  > Many learners are proud of their streak and highly averse to losing it.
- [fun] Betting in-game currency on a 7 day streak raised Day-7 retention 14%. ^f3
  > Day-7 retention showing the greatest improvement at +14%
- [fun] A way to skip weekends without losing the streak made learners 4% more likely to
  return a week later. ^f4
  > learners offered the Weekend Amulet were 4% more likely to come back a week later and 5% less likely to lose their streak
- [learning] [fun] Binge learners quit more than paced ones; streaks push little and
  often. ^f5
  > learners who binge on Duolingo lessons were much more likely to abandon the app than learners who pace themselves appropriately
- [fun] (inferred) For meio.lol: a daily draft puzzle with a streak (no account, browser
  storage) is cheap; forgiving breaks matters as much as the streak itself. ^f6
