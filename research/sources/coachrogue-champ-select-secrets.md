# Coach Rogue: The Champion Select Secrets ONLY PRO Players Know

Why: what does a popular coach (ex-pro, 93.9k subscribers) teach about champ select, and
which of it agrees or clashes with what meiolol's data finds and its picker does?

## Assessment

- Evidence: C. A former pro's experience (says 7 years on pro teams) plus counts from
  2024 pro games (Karthus's support partners, Rumble's) shown on screen without numbers
  in the narration. No solo queue data for any solo queue claim. 506k views, 17.9k likes.
- Reasoning: fair. Each rule has a mechanism (a gank needs damage and crowd control; an
  ultimate needs others to hold enemies in it; blind picks get "counter-picked by your
  own teammates"), but none is sized, and pro pick frequency is used as evidence for solo
  queue win rate.
- Interests: sells coaching (Metafy) and Patreon; a 20-second Patreon plug at 7:57.
- Weigh: the bot lane duo point matches our data; the jungler-and-laner crowd control
  rule and jungler-support synergy are testable and our data so far doesn't see them.
  Auto captions mishear names: "carus"/"carthus" is Karthus, "leid"/"Leen" is Lee Sin,
  "viiga" Veigar, "Javin" Jarvan, "filios" Aphelios, "soda" Soraka.

## Findings

- [draft] Claims the usual comp checklist (mixed damage, frontline, wave clear, engage) is surface level and misses most of what matters. @0:00 ^f1
  > the best team comps have mixed damage types good front line wave clear and engage it just doesn't need to be repeated
- [draft] Claims two-champion synergies are the most important drafting element: a gank needs damage and crowd control, so a jungler with no CC needs lanes with CC (his Lee Sin example with CC-less Rumble and Veigar). @1:08 ^f2
  > this team composition lacks the most important element when it comes to drafting individual Champion synergies
  > you need two things to make a gank work damage and crowd control
- [draft] (claim) Says these synergies matter at every rank: even iron players gank better with CC in the 2v2. @1:08 ^f3
  > iron players regardless of how bad they are at the game are far more likely to execute a successful gank if they have some crowd control
- [draft] In pro play names jungler plus support as the most important pairing (early 3v3 fights), backed by 2024 major-region pro counts: Karthus's supports are all engage, Karthus with Lulu once in ten years. @2:21 ^f4
  > in Pro play the most important two Champion Synergy that exists for your entire team is that between your jungler and your support
  > Lulu carus has only ever appeared on the same team one single time and they lost
- [draft] Ultimate synergies: champions with zone ultimates (Rumble) pair with teammates whose ultimates hold enemies in place; pro data shows Rumble with engage supports, Nautilus top. @3:29 ^f5
  > Rumble synergizes with in his best comboed with teammates who have ultimates that have Crow control or walls
- [draft] Bot lane duo fit: Caitlyn needs a poke support, Samira an engage support; mismatched pairs make bot lane not work. @3:29 ^f6
  > Caitlyn is a champion who can only play with a poke support yet she has a support who can only all it
- [draft] Defines a "neutral" pick as one that works with any partner type (Jinx with engage or enchanter supports); blind-picking one-dimensional champions lets your own teammates counter you. @5:04 ^f7
  > the champion you pick can be counterpicked not only by your opponent but also by your teammates
  > open up with a neutral Champion something like a filios or Jinx
- [draft] Advice: keep at least one "multi-tool" champion in your pool that fits any comp. @6:34 ^f8
  > you want to make sure you have at least one thing in your champion pool like this that can kind of be a multi-tool
- [draft] Pro pick order: open with the patch's strongest champion or a flex, then ADC or jungler, because jungle counters matter little and the bot matchup is decided mostly by the supports; then copy the enemy's roles to counter down the draft. @6:54 ^f9
  > jungle counter picks don't matter too much because you don't directly Lan in against your counterpart
  > most of what decides a bot Lan's matchup is the support matchup not the edc's
- [draft] Committing early to one comp style (first-pick Lulu) shrinks your team's options and invites stylistic counters. @6:54 ^f10
  > committing to a specific style of team composition too early into the draft is generally a pretty bad thing to do
- [draft] Stylistic counters: some picks are situational (Vayne into four melee tanks); Darius, immobile, is unplayable into long-range CC comps and strong into short-range ones. @7:57 ^f11
  > picking darus against short-range enemy compositions is just heaven on Earth
- [draft] Stylistic synergy: a weak-into-range pick works when the team out-ranges the enemy, forcing them to dive onto it (BDS's Darius, Worlds 2024; his old team's Gwen in front of Kog'Maw and Lulu). @9:27 @10:57 ^f12
  > on one of my former pro teams this was the exact drafting style we used to do with Gwen instead of Darius
- [draft] [learning] Summary for solo queue: don't chase the perfect teamfight comp; blind pick a neutral champion, and when picking late pair synergies with your jungler or lane partner. @10:57 ^f13
  > you're playing solo Q your team fights will probably never group up anyway
  > make sure to Blind pick a neutral Champion that pirs well with anything your teammates might pick
