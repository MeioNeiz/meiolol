# Baseball Prospectus: Beyond the 90th Percentile

Why: whether PECOTA's published percentile range is used by writers as a frame for
"surprising" results.

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Baseball Prospectus staff column; PECOTA is a long-running system.
- Reasoning: weak for our purpose: a column, not a method piece.
- Interests: BP promoting its projections; subscription banner.
- Weigh: only the description of what PECOTA publishes is useful.

## Findings

- [ux/uncertainty] PECOTA publishes a range per player from the 10th to the 90th
  percentile, with the weighted mean as the headline. ^f1
  > PECOTA is designed to produce a wide range of projections for each player, from the 10th percentile all the way to the 90th percentile, with the weighted mean typically drawing the most attention
- [ux/uncertainty] Writers use the range's top as the line for a real surprise:
  beating the 90th percentile is the story, not beating the mean. ^f2
  > only seven hitters with 100 or more plate appearances are beating their 90th percentile
- [ux/uncertainty] (inferred) A 10th to 90th range (80%) is an established
  sports-projection convention, so meio.lol's 80% choice has precedent and can be
  named as "between the 10th and 90th percentile" on the method page. ^f3
