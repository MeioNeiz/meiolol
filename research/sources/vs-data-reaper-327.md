# Vicious Syndicate: vS Data Reaper Report #327 (first report of The Lost City of Un'Goro, 24 Jul 2025)

Why: how does Hearthstone's most cited data report handle a patch's first days, and do
its first-day numbers hold?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Running weekly since 2016 (327 reports), 2.4M games this week from
  tracker contributors, written by its long-time analysts; numbers behind the prose are
  in charts (not read), and the first-day claims are told, not tabulated.
- Reasoning: fair. Gives the mechanism for each reversal (the field changed, counters
  fell, builds improved); no intervals.
- Interests: sells memberships; none in the direction of a finding.
- Weigh: the pattern (day-one rates depend on the day-one field, nerfs can raise a deck)
  from experienced first-hand observers; sizes not given.

## Findings

- [method/dynamics] Practice: no report in a format's first days. This first report of
  the expansion is dated 24 July 2025, and the month's archive
  ([[vs-data-reaper-2025-07#^f1]]) shows the one planned a week earlier was put off
  because a balance patch landed the day after. ^f1
  > This is the first report for The Lost City of Un’Goro.
- [data] The week's sample: 2,398,000 games, 47,000 of them top 1,000 Legend. ^f2
- [method/dynamics] A day-one win rate that did not hold, with the cause in who the
  opponents were: the deck beat other new decks, and fell once players stopped playing
  those. ^f3
  > Its win rate was extremely high on the first day of the expansion, but once players stopped playing other quest decks, Quest Paladin turned out to be surprisingly mediocre.
- [method/dynamics] The same after the balance patch: the best deck on the patch's first
  day was sinking toward the bottom tier a week later as the field turned against it. ^f4
  > On day 1 of the patch, Protoss Priest was the best deck in the game across ladder. Things drastically changed since.
- [method/dynamics] The sign of a change in the notes was wrong for one card: a nerf
  made it perform better. ^f5
  > The nerf to Everburning Phoenix made the card perform better, not worse.
- [method/dynamics] Indirect effects can outweigh the direct one: a nerfed deck ended up
  better than before the patch because its counters were hit harder. ^f6
  > Quest Paladin is better now than it was just before the patch came out, as it benefitted more from the decline of its counters than the nerfs hurt it.
- [method/dynamics] Thin new archetypes are still rated, in words, with the sample named
  as the caveat. ^f7
  > Based on its small sample size, we see a lot of promise for the archetype. We estimate it is, at least, a Tier 2 performer, but it could be Tier 1.
- [method/dynamics] Checked, not there: no stated minimum games, no weighting of
  pre-patch games, no forecast of where a rate will settle beyond "sinking" or "may not
  survive next week". ^f8
- [method/dynamics] (inferred) For us: a card game's field turns over far faster than
  League's (a deck's rate is mostly its matchups), so the day-one reversals are larger
  there; the transferable part is that a day-0 rate is conditional on the day-0 mix of
  opponents and builds, which our champion-role offset does not model. ^f9
