# GOV.UK writing guidelines: Use clear language

Why: numeric sentence and paragraph limits from a mature content design team.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. The Government Digital Service's standard for all of GOV.UK, many
  publishers across departments; cites research for the specialist claim.
- Reasoning: strong. Each rule has a reason and exceptions (passive voice, jargon).
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: numbers are house rules for prose; for UI labels they are upper bounds.

## Findings

- [ux] Sentence and paragraph limits: at most 5 sentences a paragraph, split sentences
  over 25 words. Keep paragraphs and sentences short ^f1
  > Paragraphs should have no more than 5 sentences each.
  > Try to split up sentences that are over 25 words long.
- [ux] No page length limit; jargon makes even one paragraph too long. Keep paragraphs and sentences short ^f2
  > There's no maximum or minimum overall page length.
  > If you write only a single paragraph but it's full of jargon, then it's too long.
- [ux] Experts prefer plain English too: 80% preferred clear sentences in a legal study,
  more so for complex issues and more educated readers. Write clearly for specialists too ^f3
  > 80% of people preferred sentences written in clear English
  > the more educated the person and the more specialist their knowledge, the greater their preference for plain English
- [ux] Specialist terms are allowed if explained at first use; use the users' own words
  (found from search terms). Use specialist language if needed ^f4
  > Where you need to use specialist terms, you can. They're not always jargon. You just need to explain what they mean the first time you use them.
- [ux] Short words over long ones; words ending -ion and -ment lengthen sentences. Know about words to avoid ^f5
  > Words ending in ‘–ion’ and ‘–ment’ tend to make sentences longer and more complicated than they need to be.
- [web/qa] A tool is named for checking: the Hemingway app for passive voice. Use the active voice ^f6
  > You can use tools such as the Hemingway app to check if your writing needs to be more active.
- [ux] (inferred) Players who know League jargon (GD@15, CS) still read faster with plain
  labels; jargon stays where players use it, explained once (tooltip or legend). ^f7
