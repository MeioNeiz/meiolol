# Duolingo blog: Improving the streak: Forming habits one lesson at a time (Nov 2020)

Why: does a streak need a high bar, and how does a goal interact with it?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. First-hand A/B test results at Duolingo scale, relative changes only, no
  sample sizes.
- Reasoning: strong. States the data that prompted it, the test, a negative side
  effect, and a year-later check.
- Interests: company blog.
- Weigh: direction and the trade-off are the lesson; numbers are Duolingo's.

## Findings

- [retention] Learners on a 7-day streak were 2.4 times as likely to come back the next
  day as learners without a streak (correlation, not a test). ^f1
  > learners who reach a streak of 7 are 2.4 times more likely to continue using Duolingo the next day than learners without a streak
- [retention] A demanding goal blocked the habit: learners with the highest daily goal
  were least likely to be on a streak even when active every day. ^f2
  > Establishing a goal appeared to be a barrier to forming a daily habit
- [retention] Splitting the streak (one lesson keeps it) from the daily goal raised Day-14
  retention 3.3% and daily actives 1% (relative), and learners on a streak 10.5% in 20
  days. ^f3
  > separating the two mechanics led to a 3.3% increase in Day 14 retention
- [retention] The cost: fewer learners reached their daily goal; they judged a low bar to a
  habit worth more early on than amount per day. ^f4
  > fewer learners were actually reaching their daily goals without the motivation of the streak
  > lowering the barriers to building a consistent daily habit is more important
- [retention] A year later just over half of daily learners had a 7+ day streak, up from
  about a third. ^f5
  > just over half of our daily learners have a streak at least 7 days long
- [retention] (inferred) For meio.lol: a streak or weekly mark should count the smallest
  action (one Daily answer, one game looked at), with any goal shown apart from it. ^f6
