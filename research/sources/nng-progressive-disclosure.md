# NN/g: Progressive Disclosure (Nielsen, 2006)

Why: how to move explanation and options off the first view without hiding what people
need; the rule behind folding method text and filters on meio.lol.

## Assessment

- Evidence: A. The canonical reference by the recognised expert; the pattern is "more
  than 30 years old" in his words, still cited by every design system.
- Reasoning: strong. Names the trade-off, the two failure modes, costs of extra levels.
- Interests: NN/g sells training (course links on the page); the content is not a pitch.
- Weigh: trust the principles; "2 levels" is experience, not a measured threshold.

## Findings

- [ux/layout] Definition: show a few of the most important options first, the rest on
  request. Intro ^f1
  > Initially, show users only a few of the most important options.
- [ux/layout] Being on the first view itself signals importance. Benefits ^f2
  > the very fact that something appears on the initial display tells users that it's important.
- [ux/layout] Claimed gains: learnability, efficiency and error rate; experts gain too by
  not scanning past rarely used features. Benefits ^f3
  > Progressive disclosure thus improves 3 of usability's 5 components: learnability, efficiency of use, and error rate.
- [ux/layout] First of two rules: everything frequently needed goes up front, and the
  first view must not be crowded. Usability Criteria ^f4
  > You have to disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions.
- [ux/layout] Second rule: the way to the detail must be obvious and its label must say
  what is behind it (information scent). Usability Criteria ^f5
  > label the button or link in a way that sets clear expectations for what users will find when they progress to the next level.
- [ux/layout] Limit: more than two disclosure levels usually tests badly. Usability Criteria ^f6
  > In practice, designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability because users often get lost when moving between the levels.
- [ux/layout] Information-rich sites and mobile benefit as much as applications.
  Applications vs. Websites ^f7
  > Websites have grown so complex that progressive disclosure is a good idea for many information-rich sites as well.
- [ux/layout] Things used together with back-and-forth belong on one screen, not split
  (hotel example); split only what is used at a different stage. Staged Disclosure ^f8
  > People use all of these features together, with frequent back-and-forth modifications, so they should be on 1 screen.
- [ux/layout] (inferred) For meio.lol: the number and its game count stay visible
  (frequently needed), method prose and rare filters go one level down behind a label
  naming what is there, never a second nested level. ^f9
