# Milne-Ives et al. 2023: Potential associations between behavior change techniques and engagement with mobile health apps (systematic review)

Why: which kinds of features (goals, progress, feedback, prompts, social) go with people
coming back to a self-improvement app?

## Assessment

- Evidence: B. Peer-reviewed systematic review, 28 studies, but associations coded by
  the authors from varied outcome measures; no meta-analysis.
- Reasoning: fair. States clearly it only hypothesises associations for testing.
- Interests: none.
- Weigh: health apps, not games; the six-technique list is the stable part.

## Findings

- [retention] Six techniques were repeatedly associated with engagement: goal setting,
  self-monitoring, feedback on behaviour, prompts or cues, rewards, social support. ^f1
  > Six BCTs were repeatedly associated with user engagement: goal setting, self-monitoring of behavior, feedback on behavior, prompts/cues, rewards, and social support.
- [retention] The evidence is associational: no meta-analysis was possible and links were
  coded by the authors, so the list is hypotheses for testing. ^f2
  > A quantitative meta-analysis of effect was not possible due to outcome variety
  > this review represents a starting point for furt
- [retention] In one included study the most-used features were aims (goal setting),
  a social wall and a virtual coach. ^f3
  > App features with the highest engagement across the different profiles were aims (BCT = goal setting), social wall (BCT = social support and demonstration of behaviors), and virtual coach (BCT = social support)
- [retention] Users asked for reminders, feedback on goal progress and personalisable
  goals. ^f4
  > Focus groups and literature review identified preferences for reminders, feedback about goal progress, messages focusing on positive emotional outcomes, personalisable goals, and instruction videos
