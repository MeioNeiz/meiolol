# How much does pick phase matter?

[STRATZ](https://stratz.medium.com/?source=post_page---byline--c87207eb5ebc-----------------------------------------)

3 min read

·

Dec 22, 2017

The general consensus prevalent in all-pick matches, is:

> Supporting heroes should be picked earlier in the draft. Core heroes picked last.

The logic behind this being that earlier picks are at greater risk of being heavily countered. Allowing the enemies to counter whoever holds 25% of your teams networth, is a worse situation than them countering your poor support with only 10% team networth.

### Sidenote on top 5 counters in Dota

Analysing every hero matchup in Dota 2, these 5 come out on top.

Advantage is calculated by estimating the **expected** winrate of hero X against hero Y based upon their individual average winrates. Then subtracting this from the **true** matchup winrate. \[The Matchup and Overall winrates are the win % of the hero in the Advantaged column\]

Some core heroes have obvious counters they genuinely struggle against, e.g. [Phantom Lancer](https://stratz.com/heroes/12) against [Timbersaw](https://stratz.com/heroes/98):

-   Timbersaw has burst area-of-effect spells to easily destory all PL illusions.
-   Timberchain allows him to chase down a retreating PL.
-   Reactive armour greatly diminishes the damage PL can deal to Timber

Other core heroes are more stable, having minor counters, yet no ridiculously bad matchups, e.g. [Juggernaut](https://stratz.com/heroes/8). His worst matchup, against [Ursa](https://stratz.com/heroes/70), is still under 3% disadvantage.

Therefore, despite _cores should pick last_ being solid advice in general, this becomes a less important match-result factor when sticking to more stable cores.

As a core player, you always want to get last pick. However all-pick Dota is far from an ideal world. If teammates are afk, or refusing to pick, sometimes you simply have to first phase pick, whatever your role. Therefore it’s important to understand which heroes you can opt for in these situations, and which should be avoided.

### Heroes to avoid first pick

Mainly cores who have obvious counters. I.e. a first pick Meepo is heavily at risk of having to play against Winter Wyvern, Sven or Earthshaker. Who can all pretty much solo kill a farmed Meepo.

### Heroes to favour first pick

Morphling includes some pre-7.07d matches. With his recent nerf he should no longer be a solid 1st phase pick.

Shadow fiend, Storm Spirit and Ember Spirit appear higher than one would expect. Being high skill-cap heroes, these 3 are often a _‘favourite’_ pick for many mid-laners. Often a player will only 1st pick their mid-hero if they are supremely confident and experienced with it. Therefore the simplest theory for the high 1st pick win-rates on these mid heroes is:

> They are not winning more because they 1st pick Shadow Fiend or Ember.
> 
> They are 1st picking Shadow Fiend or Ember because they are experienced and win more with it!

### Heroes who really don’t care

These are the heroes who have the lowest standard deviation of win-rate at different pick phases. Standard deviation is an average of how far away from the average winrate each phase is. Therefore for a 49% winrate hero, to have a low standard deviation, they would have roughly 49% winrate at every single pick phase.

A common theme amongst these heroes that can be picked whenever, is they often have strong tower push (Pugna, Shadow Shaman, Venomancer, Jakiro), or they have solid teamfighting capabilities with Area-of-Effect damage (Magnus, Kunkka, Gyro).
