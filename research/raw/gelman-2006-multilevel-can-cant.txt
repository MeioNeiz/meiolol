What It Can and Cannot Do

                       Multilevel (hierarchical) modeling is a generalization of linear and generalized linear modeling in which
                       regression coefficients are themselves given a model, whose parameters are also estimated from data.
                       We illustrate the strengths and limitations of multilevel modeling through an example of the prediction of
                       home radon levels in U.S. counties. The multilevel model is highly effective for predictions at both levels
                       of the model, but could easily be misinterpreted for causal inference.

                       KEY WORDS: Contextual effects; Hierarchical model; Multilevel regression.
