# Deployments reference

Deployments are attempts to build and deliver your [service](https://docs.railway.com/services).

All deployments will appear in the deployments view on your selected service.

## [How it works](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#how-it-works)

Upon service creation, or when changes are detected in the service source, Railway will build the service and package it into a container with [Railpack](https://docs.railway.com/builds/railpack) or a [Dockerfile](https://docs.railway.com/builds/dockerfiles) if present. If the source is a Docker Image, the build step is skipped.

Railway then starts the service using either the detected or configured [Start Command](https://docs.railway.com/builds/build-and-start-commands).

This cycle represents a deployment in Railway.

## [Deployment states](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#deployment-states)

A comprehensive up to date list of statues can be found in [Railway's GraphQL playground](https://railway.com/graphiql) under DeploymentStatus ([screenshot](https://res.cloudinary.com/railway/image/upload/v1737950391/docs/deploy-statuses.png)).

Deployments can be in any of the following states:

#### [Initializing](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#initializing)

Every Deployment in Railway begins as `Initializing` - once it has been accepted into Railway's build queue, the status will change to `Building`.

#### [Building](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#building)

While a Deployment is `Building`, Railway will attempt to create a deployable Docker image containing your code and configuration (see [Builds](https://docs.railway.com/builds)).

#### [Deploying](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#deploying)

Once the build succeeds, Railway will attempt to deploy your image and the Deployment's status becomes `Deploying`. If a [healthcheck](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/healthchecks) is configured, Railway will wait for it to succeed before proceeding to the next step.

#### [Failed](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#failed)

If an error occurs during the build or deploy process, the Deployment will stop and the status will become `Failed`.

#### [Active](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#active)

Railway will determine the deployment's active state with the following logic -

-   If the deployment **has** a [healthcheck](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/healthchecks) configured, Railway will mark the deployment as `Active` when the healthcheck succeeds.
    
-   If the deployment **does not** have a healthcheck configured, Railway will mark the deployment as `Active` after starting the container.
    

#### [Completed](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#completed)

This is the status of the Deployment when the running app exits with a zero exit code.

#### [Crashed](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#crashed)

A Deployment will remain in the `Active` state unless it [crashes](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/deployment-actions#restart-a-crashed-deployment), at which point it will become `Crashed`.

#### [Removed](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#removed)

When a new [Deployment](https://docs.railway.com/overview/the-basics#deployments) is triggered, older deploys in a `Active`, `Completed`, or a `Crashed` state are eventually removed - first having their status updated to `Removing` before they are finally `Removed`. Deployments may also be [removed manually](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#remove).

The time from when a new deployment becomes `Active` until the previous deployment is removed can be controlled by setting a [`RAILWAY_DEPLOYMENT_OVERLAP_SECONDS`](https://docs.railway.com/variables/reference#user-provided-configuration-variables) [service variable](https://docs.railway.com/overview/the-basics#service-variables).

## [Deployment menu](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#deployment-menu)

The deployment menu contains actions you can take on a deployment.

**Note:** Some actions are only available on certain deployment states.

#### [View logs](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#view-logs)

Opens the deployment up to the corresponding logs, during build the build logs will be shown, during deploy the deploy logs will be shown.

#### [Restart](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#restart)

Restarts the process within the deployment's container, this is often used to bring a service back online after a crash or if you application has locked up.

#### [Redeploy](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#redeploy)

Redeploys the selected deployment.

This is often used to bring a service back online after -

-   A crash.
-   A usage limit has been reached and raised.
-   Upgrading to Hobby when trial credits were previously depleted.
-   Being demoted from Hobby to free and then upgrading again.

**Notes** -

-   The redeploy will use the source code from the selected deployment.
    
-   Deployments older than your [plan's retention policy](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/plans#image-retention-policy) cannot be restored via rollback, and thus the rollback option will not be visible.
    

#### [Rollback](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#rollback)

Redeploys the selected deployment.

**Notes** -

-   The rollback will use the source code from the selected deployment.
    
-   Deployments older than your [plan's retention policy](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/plans#image-retention-policy) cannot be restored via rollback, and thus the rollback option will not be visible.
    

#### [Remove](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#remove)

Stops the currently running deployment, this also marks the deployment as `REMOVED` and moves it into the history section.

#### [Abort](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#abort)

Cancels the selected [initializing](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#initializing) or [building](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#building) deployment, this also marks the deployment as `REMOVED` and moves it into the history section.

## [Ephemeral storage](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#ephemeral-storage)

Every service deployment has access to 1GB of ephemeral storage on the Free plan and 100GB on a paid plan. If a service deployment exceeds the maximum allowed storage, it can be forcefully stopped and redeployed.

If your service requires data to persist between deployments, or needs more than the maximum allowed storage, you should add a [volume](https://docs.railway.com/volumes).

## [Singleton deploys](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#singleton-deploys)

By default, Railway maintains only one deploy per service.

In practice, this means that if you trigger a new deploy either [manually](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/deployment-actions#redeploy) or [automatically](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/github-autodeploys), the old version will be stopped and removed with a slight overlap for zero downtime.

Once the new deployment is online, the old deployment is sent a SIGTERM signal. By default, it is given 0 seconds to gracefully shutdown before being forcefully stopped with a SIGKILL. We do not send any other signals under any circumstances.

The time given to gracefully shutdown can be controlled by setting a [`RAILWAY_DEPLOYMENT_DRAINING_SECONDS`](https://docs.railway.com/variables/reference#user-provided-configuration-variables) [service variable](https://docs.railway.com/overview/the-basics#service-variables).

## [Railway initiated deployments](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#railway-initiated-deployments)

Occasionally, Railway will initiate a new deployment to migrate your service from one host to another. This may happen for one of three reasons:

1.  At your plan tier, such as Trial or Hobby, you may be pre-emptively moved to a different host to help us optimize workload distribution.
2.  A host requires security or performance updates and requires there to be no running workloads on the machine. We provide advance warning for these events.
3.  A host has a fault and we migrate workloads off the machine to another to maintain customer service continutity.

We perform these migrations when implementing security patches or platform upgrades to the underlying infrastructure where your service was previously running. During platform-wide upgrades, your service might be redeployed multiple times as we roll out changes across Railway's infrastructure. These deployments are mandatory and cannot be opted out of.

These Railway-initiated deployments will display with a banner above the Active deployment to clearly identify them.

## [Deployments paused - limited access](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#deployments-paused---limited-access)

Railway's core offering is dynamic, allowing you to vertically or horizontally scale with little-to-no-notice. To offer this flexibility to customers, Railway takes the stance that Pro/Enterprise tiers may, in rare occasions, be prioritized above Free/Hobby tiers.

During periods where Pro/Enterprise users require additional resources, Railway may temporarily suspend resource allocation, including builds, to Free, and more rarely Hobby, customers.

### [During a pause](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#during-a-pause)

-   You'll see a "Limited Access" indicator in your dashboard
-   New deployments will be queued rather than immediately processed
-   All other Railway features remain fully functional
-   No data or existing deployments are affected

### [Continue deploying during high traffic](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#continue-deploying-during-high-traffic)

If you need to deploy immediately during a high traffic pause, you can upgrade to the Pro plan to bypass the deployment queue. Pro tier customers are not affected by deployment pausing and can continue deploying normally during high traffic periods.

### [When normal operations resume](https://docs.railway.com/deployments/reference#when-normal-operations-resume)

-   Queued deployments will automatically process in order
-   You'll receive a notification when deployment capabilities are restored
-   No action is required on your part
