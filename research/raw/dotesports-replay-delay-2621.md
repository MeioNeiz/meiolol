# Riot delays League’s private replay changes after community backlash

The replay lockout waits.

[Rijit Banerjee](https://dotesports.com/author/rijitbanerjee)

|

Last Updated: Oct 8, 2026 4:33 AM GMT+1

Riot Games has delayed its controversial changes to _League of Legends_’ replay system after a wave of concern from players, coaches, analysts, and content creators.

The changes, introduced alongside the current update, will now ship in _League_ Patch 26.21. Riot Games says it is working on a solution for creators and coaches who rely on access to other players’ replays before the restrictions go live. 

“The announced changes to replays are being delayed to Patch 26.21,” Riot said on X. The delay comes only days after the developer announced that [_League_](https://dotesports.com/league-of-legends) replays would become effectively private.

## What is Riot changing about League replays?

Under the original Patch 26.20 changes, players would only be able to access replays from matches they personally played in. That means you could still download your own replay from the _League_ client and use it to review your performance, but you would no longer be able to download a replay from a match involving other players.

Image via Riot Games

Third-party applications and websites would also lose access to replay downloads entirely. 

The developer stressed that the change would not prevent players from reviewing their own games. The controversial part was the loss of access to other people’s games. That distinction is important because _League’s_ replay system has developed into much more than a way for players to rewatch their own mistakes.

For years, downloading another player’s replay has been one of the simplest ways for _League_ players to study how better players approach the game.

An aspiring top laner could download a Challenger or professional player’s game and examine how they handled a specific matchup. A jungler could study pathing and timing. A support player could examine warding and roaming decisions.

Unlike a conventional VOD, a replay provides a much more interactive way of studying a game. Viewers can move around the match, change perspectives, and inspect specific moments rather than simply watching a fixed recording.

That made replays particularly useful for players trying to improve, as well as coaches and analysts studying opponents or potential players. Former professional player Marcin “Jankos” Jankowski also pointed to replays being used to improve individual gameplay, analyze jungle pathing, and help coaches and analysts study players. 

An ordinary player who wants to learn a champion can use a high-level replay to see how an expert handles a matchup. An aspiring amateur can study players above their own level. A coach can analyse a player’s decision-making. An analyst can break down a particular strategy.

That ecosystem developed around the ability to access games that you did not personally play.

## Replays also became a major part of League content

The change also threatened an entire category of _League_ content. Creators have built channels around analyzing professional and high-ranked solo queue games, while montage and educational creators have relied on replay files to produce footage. That is why some of the strongest reactions came from content creators who realized that their existing workflows could disappear almost immediately.

Other creators similarly warned that losing access to professional and high-ranked replays would make their existing formats considerably more difficult or impossible. 

The developer is not removing the ability to watch _League_ gameplay. Professional matches will still be broadcast, and players can still watch streams and uploaded VODs.

The flexibility of having the underlying replay file disappears. That matters for educational content because a creator working with a replay can pause, change perspectives, and inspect the game in ways that are not always possible with a conventional broadcast or YouTube video.

## Riot’s security concerns are behind the change

Image via Riot Games

Riot did not introduce private replays simply to restrict content creators. The change is part of a much larger security overhaul affecting Riot’s games.

They began proactively blocking unknown third-party applications from accessing game memory by default across _League of Legends_, _Teamfight Tactics_, and _VALORANT_.  Approved third-party tools have been given until April 2027 to transition affected functionality to Riot’s official APIs.

The developer says third-party applications historically had access to game memory for legitimate purposes such as trackers, overlays, and mods. However, the company says that same access has increasingly been abused to “develop cheats, bot farms and other unauthorized tools.”

The company specifically [pointed](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/riot/performance/game-memory-access-removed-for-third-party-apps) to the growth of AI-assisted reverse engineering, arguing that it has lowered the barrier for people attempting to develop these tools at scale. The developer’s position is that the problem has become large enough that simply policing individual applications is no longer sufficient. Instead, it wants official APIs to become the only approved way for third-party applications to retrieve game data.

The replay restriction is part of that broader effort to close avenues that can be exploited by bad actors. But the same infrastructure has also supported legitimate uses that players have come to depend on.

If Riot creates a system specifically for professional teams, coaches, and established creators, much of the existing esports and educational ecosystem could continue. But that would not necessarily restore the experience for an ordinary player who wants to download a Challenger player’s replay and study it.

That distinction could become one of the biggest points of discussion when Riot explains its Patch 26.21 solution. The company also believes the security changes will ultimately reduce cheating, crashes, and other problems associated with unauthorized tools. The delay is a response to the backlash, but it does not settle the larger debate. Patch 26.21 will show whether the company can find that middle ground.
